## Why black

Black makes colour look brighter and lines look sharper. For data-heavy work, it lets thin strokes and small labels stand out without needing to be thick or loud.

## Fewer colours, more contrast

On a dark background, every colour fights for attention. I keep a small palette and give each colour one job: one for highlights, a few for categories, and white or grey for text.

## Use one warm highlight

A single warm colour, like gold, works as a spotlight. Use it for the title and the most important numbers, and nowhere else. If everything is highlighted, nothing is.

## Add light sources

Soft glowing shapes, like a blurred yellow circle, give a flat black canvas depth. They guide the eye through the layout without adding more information.

## Watch the small text

Small, light text on black can disappear when printed. Test a print early, and make captions a little larger and bolder than they look on screen.

## Texture helps

Pure black can feel empty. Subtle grain, faded background charts, or a mockup with shadow give the canvas a physical feel, as in the cover of my [State of Global Air poster](/work/state-of-global-air-report).
