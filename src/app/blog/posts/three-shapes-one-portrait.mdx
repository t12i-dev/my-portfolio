## Take colour away first

The first step for the [Shapes & Sound collage](/work/collage-portrait) was removing colour from the photograph. A monochrome face on warm, paper-like texture gives the shapes on top a clean stage. If the photo keeps its colour, the shapes have to compete with it.

## Limit the palette

I used only three colours: red, orange and a muted teal. A small palette forces each shape to earn its place. It also gives the piece a poster-like feel, close to the mid-century prints I like.

## Make the viewer compare

The most important decision was framing the two eyes differently. One sits inside a solid orange block; the other inside a thin red outline. Because they don't match, the eye keeps moving between them, and that movement is what makes the portrait feel alive.

## Leave room

Not every part of the face is covered. The mouth, the nose and most of the headphones are untouched. Empty space is what makes the shapes feel placed rather than scattered.

## Try it yourself

Take any portrait, convert it to black and white, choose three colours, and add no more than five shapes. The constraint is the point.
