## Overview

*State of Global Air* is an infographic poster built on data from the State of Global Air 2025 report, which analyses air quality and its health impacts for countries around the world. The goal was to take a dense, number-heavy report and turn it into one poster that rewards a quick glance and a long read.

## The challenge

Air pollution data is usually presented as tables and line charts: accurate, but easy to scroll past. The report covers many dimensions at once: risk factors, causes of death, gender, years and countries. The poster had to show all of that without overwhelming the viewer, and still feel like something you would want to hang on a wall.

## Approach

### A dark canvas

The poster sits on a black background so the data can glow. Warm golds highlight titles and key numbers, while soft pinks, lilacs and blues separate categories. The result reads almost like a night sky, which fits a subject about the air we breathe.

### One chart type per question

Each section answers a single question and uses the form that suits it best:

- **Risk factors, deaths, year:** radial charts compare non-communicable diseases between Vietnam and Singapore from 2014 to 2023, and show deaths in Thailand by risk factor.
- **Deaths by gender:** each year from 2019 to 2023 in Vietnam becomes a pair of organic, flower-like glyphs, pink and blue, sized by the number of deaths.
- **Southeast Asia:** stacked columns show weighted annual average PM2.5 concentrations across the region's countries, next to an area chart ranking risk factors by total deaths.
- **Environmental risks:** a flow chart traces deaths linked to environmental and occupational risks in Vietnam across 2019–2023.

### Typography as the anchor

The title, *State of Global Air Report*, is set in a large, high-contrast serif in gold, with "Air" pushed to an oversized italic. It anchors the busy lower half of the poster and gives the eye a clear place to start.

### Layered reading

The poster is meant to be read in layers. From a distance you see the title and the shapes. Closer up you find legends, axis labels and short captions that explain what each chart measures. A QR code links viewers to more information.

## Outcome

The final piece brings risk factors, causes of death, gender, time and geography together in one composition. It shows how data visualisation can be both rigorous and expressive: every shape is driven by the numbers, but the poster still works as an image first.
