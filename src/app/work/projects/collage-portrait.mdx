## Overview

*Shapes & Sound* is a collage portrait that combines a black-and-white photograph with flat geometric shapes. The figure wears headphones, and the shapes act like visual rhythm laid over the face: blocks, circles and frames that pull your attention from one eye to the other.

## Concept

The idea was to treat a portrait like a poster. Instead of retouching the face, the composition adds a second layer on top of it, made of simple forms in a limited palette. The photograph supplies texture and emotion, and the shapes supply structure.

## Process

### A monochrome base

The portrait is desaturated and given a printed, slightly grainy texture on a warm paper-coloured background. Removing colour from the face lets the added shapes carry all of the colour in the piece.

### A limited palette

Only a few colours are used: a deep red, a warm orange and a muted teal, all inspired by mid-century poster design. Each shape is either solid or transparent, so the face stays visible underneath.

### Framing the eyes

The two eyes are framed differently: one inside a solid orange block with a small circular cut-out, the other inside a thin red outline over a red tint. The asymmetry makes the viewer compare them, which gives the portrait its tension.

## Outcome

The finished piece sits between photography and graphic design. It is a small study in how a few flat shapes can change the way a familiar face is read.
